# GST 112 Repository Analysis: Five Highest-Yield Exam Topics

**Repository analysed:** <https://github.com/dbolaji1/GST-112.git>  
**Course materials reviewed:** lecture notes, PDF slide decks, weekly summaries, full-course summary, case studies, reflection questions, and the main course manual.  

**Method used:** Topics were ranked by frequency across files, amount of coverage, presence in learning objectives/case studies/summaries, explicit revision prompts such as questions to ponder, and links across multiple weeks or chapters.

> **Note on page/slide references:** For ordinary PDFs, the listed number is the PDF page number. For PowerPoint files exported as PDFs, each PDF page was treated as the slide number. Where a page number is not meaningful beyond a slide deck, the closest slide title or section heading is also given.

## Study Priority

### 1. Nation-building, national integration, state creation, and Nigeria’s political evolution

This should be studied first because it is the most connected topic. It appears in Week 1, Week 5, the full-course summary, the main course manual, and multiple slide decks on parties, independence, civil war, and military rule. It also absorbs repeatedly tested ideas such as colonialism, ethnicity, state creation, civil war, the 3Rs, and national unity.

### 2. Culture, ethnic diversity, geo-political zones, and intergroup relations

This is the foundation of the course. The main course manual devotes Sessions 1–4 to it, and the repository includes a dedicated intergroup-relations lecture. It also explains why nation-building is difficult in Nigeria.

### 3. Portuguese incursion, slave trade, legitimate trade, and colonial occupation

This is high priority because it has two full lecture decks, a Week 3 summary, learning objectives, quizzes/questions, and full-course summary case-study coverage. It also explains the transition from external trade to exploitation and colonial rule.

### 4. Trade, economic transformation, and modern economic realities

This topic appears in Week 6, the modern-economy lecture, and the main manual’s sessions on trade, economic self-reliance, and national development. It links precolonial exchange to modern problems such as oil dependence, corruption, SAP, poverty, and diversification.

### 5. Democracy, rule of law, human rights, citizenship, and youth political awakening

This remains high-yield because it spans Weeks 7, 8, and 10 and includes current issues such as police brutality, #EndSARS, and youth political participation. It is slightly lower than the first four because its coverage is more concentrated in later weekly materials, though it remains very examinable.

- **Trade, commerce, and economic disruption**

- **Citizenship and constitutional duties**
- **Human rights and rule of law**
- **Youth political participation and #EndSARS**

## Quick Exam Strategy

If time is limited, revise in this order:

1. **Nation-building/state creation/civil war/military rule**
2. **Culture, ethnic groups, and intergroup relations**
3. **Slave trade, legitimate trade, and colonial occupation**
4. **Trade and economic realities**
5. **Human rights, citizenship, rule of law, and #EndSARS**

Focus especially on definitions, causes, consequences, named policies, named organisations, and examples given in the slides and summaries.
